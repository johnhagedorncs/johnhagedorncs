# John Hagedorn

Industrial time-series anomaly detection: ML pipelines, the infrastructure they run on, and
the statistics that decide whether a result is real.

Three projects on machine condition monitoring: detecting that a rotating machine
is degrading, from vibration data, before it fails.

Most anomaly detection portfolios show a model finding an anomaly. These show the
cases where the standard approach reports something that isn't there, and what I
do about it.

## How they connect

`sensorlint` belongs at the ingest boundary of `bearing-watch`, so that a truncated
file or a stream of raw converter counts aborts the run instead of quietly
producing a plausible anomaly score. Wiring it in is the next step.

`tsad-harness` proves that a self-referencing MAD z-score cannot detect gradual
degradation. `bearing-watch` therefore calibrates its threshold against a declared
stationary reference window instead. The harness is where the rule is tested; the
service is where it is used.

---

## The three results worth reading

**A self-referencing robust threshold cannot see a ramp at any magnitude.**
If a detector takes its median and MAD from the same series it scores, then for a
ramp of range R the scaled MAD is `0.37065·R` while the largest deviation is
`0.5·R`, so `max|z| = 1.349` and R cancels exactly. A 1000× ramp is precisely as
invisible as a 2× one, and any threshold above ~1.35 is unreachable by
construction. Measured residual against the analytic constant: 0.0, across three
orders of magnitude. Derivation and verification in
[`tsad-harness/paper/ramp_blindness.md`](https://github.com/johnhagedorncs/tsad-harness/blob/main/paper/ramp_blindness.md).

The consequence is the point: a detector built this way structurally cannot report
gradual degradation, which is the thing condition-monitoring teams most want to
know.

**A distribution-drift monitor cannot tell a failing bearing from a changed
sensor.** In [`bearing-watch`](https://github.com/johnhagedorncs/bearing-watch) (synthetic data so far), injecting a 1.8× broadband gain on
*healthy* data produces a larger drift signal (mean PSI 12.09) than the real
degradation does (6.64). The fix is a band-split discriminant: a multiplicative
gain scales every frequency band equally, so band-energy *ratios* are invariant
under a gain change but move under real wear. Measured shape z-score of 0.00 for
the gain change against 37.01 for mechanical degradation, recovering the injected
gain to 0.0% error. The README also lists five cases where this does not work.

**Random scores look like a working detector.** In the `tsad-harness` results, a
uniform random baseline scores 1.000 detection on every geometry at every
magnitude under a series-level metric. This is why the harness reports point-wise,
event-wise, and point-adjusted scores side by side, always with the random floor
in the same table.

## Negative results, kept on purpose

- The autoencoder in `bearing-watch` does **not** beat PCA reconstruction error.
  They tie, and PCA gives better separation (12.6 vs 11.1 MAD).
- The domain features do not earn their place: dropping kurtosis and the
  bearing-defect bands leaves detection unchanged with *better* separation.
- The corrected detector in `tsad-harness` fires on 50% of points during benign
  regime switches, where the broken detector fires on none. The fix is not free.
- Exponential ramps *are* detected above ~10×, so ramp blindness is about the
  uniform value distribution, not about gradualness. That is a narrower claim than
  the one I started with.

## Provenance

All data is public or synthetic. `bearing-watch` targets the NASA IMS bearing
dataset (Center for Intelligent Maintenance Systems, University of Cincinnati) and
ships a generator producing that same on-disk format, so the full pipeline runs
end to end with no download. **Every bearing-watch number quoted here is from that
synthetic generator;** rerunning on the real IMS data is in progress. `tsad-harness` is entirely synthetic by design.
Nothing here contains code, data, or results from any employer.
